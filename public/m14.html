M14 Space & Extreme Environments

Space Governance and Interplanetary Jurisdiction

Legal and ethical frameworks governing human activity in space were designed for robotic probes and brief orbital missions. Long-duration missions, commercial operations, and permanent off-Earth settlements require governance of human rights, medical ethics, crew autonomy, jurisdictional authority, and institutional accountability structures that the Outer Space Treaty (1967) and Artemis Accords (2020) do not address. Every institution planning or supporting human operations beyond Earth's atmosphere is governing in a legal vacuum — and that vacuum is a design choice.

The Governance Problem

Why This Module Exists

Legal and ethical frameworks governing human activity in space were designed for robotic probes and brief orbital missions. Long-duration missions, commercial operations, and permanent off-Earth settlements require governance of human rights, medical ethics, crew autonomy, jurisdictional authority, and institutional accountability structures that the Outer Space Treaty (1967) and Artemis Accords (2020) do not address. Every institution planning or supporting human operations beyond Earth's atmosphere is governing in a legal vacuum — and that vacuum is a design choice.

Evidence Base

The Research Foundation

Treaty on Principles Governing Activities in Outer Space (1967) · 610 U.N.T.S. 205 DOI →

Outer Space Treaty — the foundational governance document for space activity

“States bear international responsibility for national activities in space regardless of whether carried out by governmental or non-governmental entities. No provision addresses individual crew rights, medical ethics, or intra-mission governance.”

NASA (2020). The Artemis Accords: Principles for Cooperation in Peaceful Exploration DOI →

Bilateral agreements establishing norms for Moon, Mars, comet, and asteroid operations

“Addresses transparency, interoperability, heritage site protection, and orbital debris. Does not address crew rights, medical authority, conflict resolution, commercial accountability, or post-mission reintegration governance.”

Kanas, N., & Manzey, D. (2008). Space Psychology and Psychiatry (2nd ed.). Springer/Praxis. DOI →

Comprehensive review of psychological and behavioral health in space environments

“Interpersonal conflict, communication difficulties with mission control, and inadequate autonomy are among the most significant predictors of mission risk in long-duration spaceflight. None of these risk factors is addressed by current legal governance frameworks.”

Stuster, J. (2010). Behavioral Issues Associated with Long-Duration Space Expeditions. NASA TM-2010-216130. DOI →

Analysis of isolated confined environment analogs — Antarctic stations, submarines, polar expeditions — for space governance implications

“Governance structures that preserve crew autonomy, provide independent accountability mechanisms, and establish clear authority boundaries produce significantly better behavioral health outcomes in analog environments.”

Six Governance Dimensions

What the Assessment Measures

Each readiness assessment scores your institution across these six governance dimensions on a five-point scale, producing a tier report (PIONEERING, EMERGING, DEVELOPING, or EARLY STAGE).

1 Legal Framework and Jurisdictional Clarity
2 Crew Rights and Autonomy Governance
3 Medical and Clinical Governance
4 Community and Social Governance
5 Commercial and Institutional Accountability
6 Post-Mission Reintegration Governance
PIONEERING Governance

What the Highest Tier Looks Like

PIONEERING — Fully Implemented Governance

Documented legal framework addressing jurisdiction and applicable law for off-Earth operations; written crew autonomy protocol defining individual rights including the right to refuse mission directives on documented health grounds; clinical governance framework for autonomous healthcare decision-making; formal conflict resolution and privacy protocols; independent accountability mechanism external to operational command; post-mission reintegration protocol with documented health information confidentiality protections.

The full tier operationalization — PIONEERING through EARLY STAGE — is available in the Framework Foundation.

Space Law Connection

The Most Unforgiving Test Case for Human Governance

Remove every assumption our institutions were built on — gravity, atmosphere, proximity to other human beings, access to the natural world, the possibility of leaving — and what is left of law? What is left of ethics? What does psychological and physiological wellbeing even mean when every variable in the environment is constructed?

M14 is where the Nervous-System-Aware Governance framework meets its highest-stakes application. The six governance dimensions in this module are not aspirational standards for Earth-based institutions. In space, they are survival infrastructure.
