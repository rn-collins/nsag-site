About NSAG

The framework started
in a neuroscience lab.

At the University of Pennsylvania's Brain Center, measuring cognitive function in a research lab, the question that would eventually become NSAG first appeared: if we can measure what the brain is doing, why can't we measure what institutions are doing to brains?

The Origin Sequence

A Framework That Started Freshman Year

Penn Brain Center
Cognitive Neuroscience Laboratory

Work-study, first semester at the University of Pennsylvania. Measuring cognitive function. Learning to treat the brain's activity as something observable, documentable, governable. The first encounter with the gap between what a system produces and what it can see it produces.

Penn Medicine Neuropsychiatry
Internship with Ruben C. Gur, PhD and Raquel E. Gur, MD, PhD

Clinical neuroscience research. Brain imaging. The physiological reality of how environment and experience shape neural function — not metaphorically, but measurably. The intersection of neuroscience and institutional context begins to take shape.

Penn Bioethics Center
Ethics of clinical neuroscience

The governance layer. What are institutions obligated to do once they know what the brain is doing? What governance frameworks should exist for knowledge about neural function? The gap between scientific evidence and institutional accountability becomes the central question.

National Research Center for Women and Families
FDA Medical Device Safety Research

Working with Diana Zuckerman. The gap between what a medical institution knows about the safety of its tools and what it governs. The governance gap is not theoretical — it has documented, measurable consequences for specific people.

Boston University School of Medicine
MS Anatomy & Neurobiology, Brain Plasticity & Neuroimaging Laboratory (PI: Karin Schon, PhD)

The thesis work: racialized stress, allostatic load, and medial temporal lobe volumes in Black and Afro-Caribbean older adults. The physiological consequences of institutional environments on the brain — measured directly, in tissue. The science that became NSAG's foundational claim.

Northeastern University School of Law
JD Candidate 2029, NuLawLab Research Partner

The governance framework needs the legal infrastructure. Law is how institutions are held accountable. NSAG's framework is not just a scientific claim — it is a governance standard with legal and policy implications.

Brown University · AISLE Project
Advanced Legislative Scorer, Center for Technological Responsibility

Scoring AI legislation across 30+ US jurisdictions. The policy layer. What does governance actually look like when it becomes law? How does the scientific evidence base translate into enforceable institutional standards?

The New Health Institute
Director of AI Strategy (beginning July 1, 2026)

The operational layer. Building AI governance tools for health institutions. The NSAG framework applied at the intersection of AI, health system design, and institutional accountability.

What NSAG Is

The Neurocognitive Systems Advisory Group

NSAG is an advisory practice built on a single claim: every institution is already shaping how people think, feel, and participate — physiologically, not metaphorically. Through physical environment, procedural language, digital tools, disclosure architecture, and organizational structure, institutions produce measurable effects on the nervous systems of the people inside them.

Most institutions are producing these effects without measuring them, without intending them, and without governance infrastructure designed to make them intentional. That gap — between what an institution is producing and what it can see and govern — is the governance gap NSAG exists to close.

The sequence that makes everything else possible: governance must come before systems design. An institution that redesigns before it governs is redesigning in the dark. Nervous-System-Aware Governance is the infrastructure that makes the sequence possible.

Neurocognitive Systems Advisory Group

The organization. The advisory practice. The 15 modules. The governance assessment tools. The institutional relationship that makes the governance work real.

Nervous-System-Aware Governance

The framework theory. The intellectual claim that institutions govern nervous systems whether or not they intend to — and that governance infrastructure can make those effects intentional.

Aligning System Design and Governance

What it means in practice. Not designing with good intentions. Designing from a governance framework that specifies what the institution must produce, how it will measure whether it is producing it, and what accountability exists when it does not.
