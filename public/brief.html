Publication status

The Brief is not published, and this page will not pretend otherwise.

NSAG Brief was proposed as a periodical on governance and the nervous system. It does not exist. There is no subscription, no mailing list, no archive, and no first issue. Nothing on this page enrols you, charges you, renews anything, or promises delivery.

No newsletter or paid intelligence subscription is available on this deployment. If you have seen a price, a signup, or a delivery promise for the Brief anywhere, it is out of date. The current status of every proposed NSAG product is on the product register.

Why a page for something that does not exist

Because the alternative — deleting it — is worse. The Brief was named publicly, links to it exist, and a page that quietly 404s teaches a reader nothing about whether the thing was cancelled, renamed, or simply hidden. A named product that goes silent is how audiences learn to distrust everything else on a site.

There is also a more useful reason. The reason the Brief has not shipped is itself a governance problem, and it is the same one the framework describes: an institution producing an effect it has not specified, measured, or made anyone accountable for. A publication is an institution in miniature. It is worth saying out loud what it would have to satisfy first.

What the Brief would have to satisfy before a first issue

These are not aspirations. They are the conditions under which publishing would be defensible, and every one of them is currently unmet.

A stated research protocol

How a claim gets into an issue: which sources count, what a second reader checks, and what happens to a claim that cannot be traced. Without this, a periodical is an opinion column with footnotes.

A current-through date on every claim

Governance evidence rots. Regulations change, vendors are acquired, the person accountable leaves. Every factual claim has to carry the date it was last checked, or a reader eighteen months later cannot tell what they are holding.

Named authorship and declared limits

Someone who can be asked to defend a finding, and a limitations section written before the conclusions rather than appended to them.

A correction mechanism that works

A route for a reader to say “this is wrong”, a standard for what evidence a correction needs, and a public record of what changed. A publication without one accumulates errors silently.

An editorial calendar that can actually be met

A cadence promised and missed is a broken commitment to every subscriber. Capacity has to exist before the promise, not after it.

Fulfilment and failure handling

What happens when delivery fails, when someone wants to leave, when an issue is late, and what a reader is owed. Unanswered, these are the questions that turn a publication into a complaint.

Read as a list, that is unglamorous. Read as a governance audit of a proposed product, it is exactly the exercise NSAG asks institutions to run on themselves — and the honest outcome of running it here was: not yet.

What there is to read instead

The substantive material is published, free, and needs no subscription. It is a body of work rather than a product line, and it is all reachable now:

  • The framework and tier definitions — what good governance covers across six dimensions, and the documentary test at each of the four tiers.
  • Fifteen module scopes — each stating the evidence base for one institutional design problem, the six dimensions it turns on, and the implementation questions an institution would have to answer.
  • Evidence and resources and written analysis — the reading behind the modules.
  • Applied work and advisory engagements — what the framework looks like in use.
  • The operations matrix — module by module, what is public, what is retired, and what is delivered by a person.

If the Brief is ever published

It will appear here, at this address, with its research protocol and its correction policy visible before its first issue rather than after. Until then this page stays exactly as blunt as it is. There is nothing to sign up for, and no list is being collected in the meantime.
