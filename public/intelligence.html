Product register · reviewed 29 August 2026

Every named NSAG product, and exactly why it is not offered.

Compass, Brief, Index, Profile, module subscriptions, analyst access, and automated report delivery were named publicly before they existed. This register keeps every one of those names visible with its true status attached, because a name that disappears teaches a reader nothing and a name left standing without a status is a promise.

No checkout or subscription contract is active. No price shown in any earlier version of this site is presently offered, reserved, charged, renewed, cancelled, or automatically fulfilled. The fifteen module self-assessments are retired and cannot be taken; no tier report is generated.

Why the gaps are the interesting part

Each row's third column is short, and each item in it is the same kind of thing: a control that would make the product accountable to the person receiving it. That is not a coincidence. The framework NSAG publishes argues that an institution producing effects it has not specified, measured, or assigned accountability for is Early Stage governance regardless of its intentions. A product shipped without those controls would be exactly that.

So the register is not an apology for slow progress. It is the framework applied to its own author, and the outcome is legible: the analysis is ready and the delivery infrastructure is not. Publishing the analysis and withholding the infrastructure is the honest ordering of those two facts.

What a benchmark would actually require

The Index is worth expanding on, because “we benchmarked ourselves against peers” is the single most requested thing in governance work and the easiest to do badly. A defensible benchmark needs a cohort defined in advance rather than assembled from whoever responded; consent from participating institutions covering the comparison itself, not just the data collection; a minimum-cell rule so that no institution is identifiable by elimination in a small sector; a de-identification review by someone who did not build the dataset; and a published statement of the questions the benchmark cannot answer. Without all five, a benchmark launders a convenience sample into an authority claim. NSAG has none of the five, so it offers no benchmark.

The release standard

A concept moves from “not offered” to “offered” only when its audience, scope, deliverable, sample, price, capacity, billing, delivery, review, confidentiality, support, cancellation, data use, and declared limitations all agree — across this site, the operating system behind it, and whatever a customer would be told in writing. Agreement across all three is the point: most product failures of this kind are not lies but drift, where the site says one thing, the invoice says another, and the person answering email says a third.

Until a concept clears that, it stays on this register with its name intact and its status stated.
