Psych-CE Specialty Track

Psychology CE

Immordino-Yang and Damasio established in 2007 that physiological threat states suppress the prefrontal cortical systems required for higher-order cognition, learning, and emotional regulation. Barrett et al. (2015) measured the consequence: seven built environment parameters explain sixteen percent of the variation in pupils’ academic progress after controlling for teacher quality — a finding comparable to major curriculum interventions. Cantor et al. (2019) confirmed the developmental stakes: educational environments during critical periods literally shape neural architecture.

Session Psy1 gives psychologists the affective neuroscience foundation and the governance framework for institutional design consultation. The session covers the somatic marker hypothesis applied to educational and clinical environments, the seven HEAD Project parameters, experience-dependent neural development, and the NSAG M12 governance dimensions. The practice exercise applies all three evidence bases to a described graduate psychology training clinic.

Who This Is For
  • Licensed psychologists (PhD, PsyD, EdD in psychology)
  • Licensed psychological associates
  • Psychologists in postdoctoral training
  • School psychologists
  • Neuropsychologists
  • Clinical and counseling psychologists in educational, clinical, or organizational consulting roles

Sample Course Content

Practice Hypothetical & Model Analysis

The following is a representative hypothetical from this specialty track. Each session includes a practice application exercise based on a realistic institutional scenario. The model analysis below represents the level of analysis expected from practitioners completing this course.

Session Psy1 · Affective Neuroscience and Institutional Design

Hypothetical Psy1: The Middle School Consultation

A school district contacts you to consult on declining standardized test scores in its three middle schools. The district has already tried: a new math curriculum (3 years ago), extended school hours (last year), and an academic enrichment program (this year). Scores have not improved.

During your site visit, you observe: fluorescent lighting throughout with no natural light in classrooms, a rigid 45-minute period schedule with 3-minute transitions, classroom temperature averaging 74–78°F, all desks in rows with no flexible seating, and one-size-fits-all workspace for all learners regardless of sensory needs.

Discussion Questions
  1. Apply Immordino-Yang and Damasio (2007): identify two environmental conditions in this school that are likely to activate subcortical stress pathways and reduce prefrontal cortical availability for learning.
  2. Apply Barrett et al. (2015): score this school on the HEAD parameters of Ownership and Flexibility. Justify your scores.
  3. The superintendent asks: ‘If we change the building, will test scores improve?’ How do you respond accurately — neither overclaiming nor dismissing the finding?
  4. Draft a governance recommendation for the school board that you could support as a psychologist consulting to this district.
Model Analysis
  1. Affective neuroscience analysis: (a) Temperature 74–78°F: thermal discomfort activates the HPA axis and sympathetic nervous system, producing cortisol elevation and physiological arousal that competes with the parasympathetic regulation required for focused learning. (b) 3-minute transitions: insufficient time for arousal regulation between activity demands; brief unpredictable transitions may activate threat responses in students with sensory processing differences or trauma histories; insufficient for prefrontal systems to reset between competing demands.
  2. HEAD scoring: Ownership — EARLY STAGE: all-rows seating with no personalization, no student choice in learning environment, no co-creation of space. Barrett et al. identify low Ownership as one of the most significant parameters affecting the 16% variance. Flexibility — EARLY STAGE: identical workspace for all learners, no flexible seating, no accommodation for sensory or learning differences. Flexibility is among the parameters with highest individual variation effect.
  3. Evidence-based response: ‘The research by Barrett and colleagues found that seven physical environment parameters explain 16% of the variation in academic progress after controlling for teacher quality — a finding comparable to many curriculum interventions. I can say with confidence that modifying Ownership, Flexibility, and Temperature is likely to remove environmental barriers to learning. Whether test scores improve depends on whether the other factors also affecting performance are also being addressed. This is one component of a multi-component improvement strategy, and likely one of the most cost-effective relative to its effect size.’
  4. School board recommendation: ‘The Board should adopt a Classroom Environment Standard for all district middle schools, establishing minimum governance criteria: (1) Temperature maintained at 68–72°F with documented monitoring; (2) Flexible seating options available in each classroom for at least 30% of students; (3) Student-directed personalization incorporated into each classroom environment. The district should assess all classrooms against the seven HEAD Project parameters within 24 months and report findings to the Board with a targeted improvement budget request.’

This model analysis represents the depth of reasoning expected in the practice application exercise. The post-test (minimum 80%) assesses conceptual mastery of the session’s evidence base and governance framework.
