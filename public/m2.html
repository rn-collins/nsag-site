AI Legal Navigation

Algorithmic risk assessment tools now influence bail, sentencing, and parole for approximately two-thirds of the US population. Most of the people affected don't know these tools are used. Most institutions using them have no independent evaluation of their performance.

The Governance Problem

Why This Module Exists

Risk assessment algorithms are sold as objective replacements for subjective judicial bias. The evidence does not support this framing. Dressel and Farid (2018) demonstrated that COMPAS performs no better than untrained lay prediction while producing racial disparities in false-positive rates. Berk et al. (2024) showed that the bias originates in the training data — arrest records encoding decades of racially disparate policing — making technical corrections insufficient. The governance response is not to eliminate these tools but to build the institutional infrastructure that makes them accountable.

Six Governance Dimensions

What the Assessment Measures

Each readiness assessment scores your institution across these six governance dimensions on a five-point scale, producing a tier report (PIONEERING, EMERGING, DEVELOPING, or EARLY STAGE).

1 AI Tool Transparency & Explainability
2 Bias Audit & Demographic Impact Assessment
3 Human Oversight & Override Protocols
4 Plain-Language Communication to Affected Parties
5 Accountability & Error Correction
6 Community Input & Governance Structures
PIONEERING Governance

What the Highest Tier Looks Like

PIONEERING — Fully Implemented Governance

Plain-language documentation of all AI tools, publicly available; independent demographic impact assessment specific to the jurisdiction's population; override protocol with no coercive documentation burden; notification to affected individuals; error classification and remediation process; community advisory body with authority to recommend discontinuation.

The full tier operationalization — PIONEERING through EARLY STAGE, with specific observable criteria for all six dimensions — is documented in the Framework Foundation.
