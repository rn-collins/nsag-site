The full
ambition.

Fourteen fashion weeks spanning every major market — New York to Lagos, Paris to Seoul. Nine professional sports advisory targets across three continents. New York Fashion Week

New York, USA · CFDA

NYFW is the most attention-extracted fashion week in the world — and the most ungoverned. The Council of Fashion Designers of America (CFDA) runs no sustainability requirements, no diversity mandates, and no governance framework for the attention extraction infrastructure (Instagram, TikTok, celebrity seeding) that is the actual product. NSAG’s research at NYFW asks the question the CFDA has not: what is 10 days of maximum social media attention extraction doing to the nervous systems of the models, buyers, press, and audiences inside it? The CFDA diversity reports show persistent representation failures across race, size, age, and disability. At root, these diversity failures are belonging governance failures.

Active: Attention · Cognitive Load · Cultural Safety · Belonging · Economic Justice

Feb & Sept · Biannual

London Fashion Week

London, UK · British Fashion Council

LFW is the most governance-adjacent of the Big Four. The British Fashion Council (BFC) has adopted voluntary sustainability reporting, partnered with Copenhagen’s governance framework, and runs the BFC NEWGEN emerging talent programme with explicit criteria. NSAG at LFW is measuring the gap between governance claims and nervous system outcomes — between the BFC’s published sustainability commitments and what the biological evidence shows is happening to models, buyers, and press across the week. The BFC’s post-Brexit identity crisis (British fashion’s relationship to European supply chains and cultural exchange) is a live economic justice and repair governance question.

Active: Ecological Alignment · Cultural Safety · Trust · Repair & Accountability · Economic Justice

Milan Fashion Week

Milan, Italy · Camera Nazionale della Moda Italiana

Milan has the most documented cultural safety failures of any fashion week. Gucci’s blackface sweater (2019), Dolce & Gabbana’s racist China “chopsticks” campaign (2018), Prada’s blackface figurines (2018) — three major governance failures across a 24-month window, all from the world’s most prestigious luxury fashion capital. NSAG’s cultural safety dimension governs exactly what these failures represent: institutions making governance decisions about how to represent cultures without accountability structures for the nervous system harm those decisions cause. Milan is also NSAG’s most important luxury brand governance site: Gucci, Prada, Versace, Armani, Fendi — every organization a Cannes founding partner target.

Paris Fashion Week

Paris, France · Fédération de la Haute Couture et de la Mode

Paris is where the luxury economy’s governance decisions carry the most downstream consequence. LVMH’s 75+ brands — Chanel, Dior, Louis Vuitton, Saint Laurent, Balenciaga, Givenchy, Hermès — collectively govern the attention, trust, and belonging of the luxury consumer worldwide. The naming sponsorship of the Monaco Grand Prix as the “Formula 1 Louis Vuitton Grand Prix de Monaco” begins here. Balenciaga’s child imagery campaign (2022), the persistent extraction of indigenous and global cultural aesthetics without attribution — these are governance failures at maximum scale. NSAG at PFW is the same conversation as NSAG at Cannes Lions: the people who govern culture should govern it consciously.

Copenhagen Fashion Week

Copenhagen, Denmark · CPHFW Foundation

The only fashion week in the world with enforceable governance standards for participation. CPHFW’s Sustainability Requirements framework sets 18 minimum standards every brand must meet before being admitted to the schedule — covering supply chain transparency, overproduction limits, show carbon footprint, and social accountability. NSAG’s role at CPHFW is additive: Copenhagen already governs ecological and economic dimensions. NSAG provides the measurement infrastructure for the human nervous system dimensions — attention quality, belonging, emotional regulation — that CPHFW’s framework doesn’t yet cover. A research partnership with CPHFW is the most aligned fashion governance collaboration available to NSAG.

NSAG adds: Attention · Belonging · Emotional Regulation · Trust · to CPHFW’s existing ecological & economic governance

Jan & Jul · Biannual · Amsterdam

Amsterdam Fashion Week

Amsterdam, Netherlands · AMFW Foundation

Amsterdam Fashion Week has formally adopted Copenhagen’s Sustainability Requirements framework as a governance condition for participation — making it the proof-of-concept for whether a smaller, non-major fashion week can implement meaningful governance standards at scale. Amsterdam is part of the CPHFW governance network alongside Berlin, Oslo, and Helsinki. NSAG’s research at Amsterdam asks: when a fashion week that is not commercially dominant explicitly governs its ecological and economic dimensions, what happens to the human nervous system dimensions? Does formal governance at a smaller event produce better attention quality and belonging for participants than the ungoverned commercial intensity of the Big Four?

Active: Ecological Alignment · Economic Justice · Trust · Belonging · Repair & Accountability

July · Annual · Helsinki

Helsinki Fashion Week

Helsinki, Finland · Founded 2016

The world’s first explicitly sustainable fashion week — and the most NSAG-aligned fashion governance event in existence. Helsinki Fashion Week operates under mandatory zero-carbon, zero-waste, and circular economy requirements. It is completely self-financed — no government funding, no sponsorship that conflicts with its governance values. What Helsinki lacks, and what NSAG provides, is measurement of the human nervous system outcomes: does fashion that is fully governed ecologically and economically also produce measurable improvements in attention quality, emotional regulation, and belonging for participants? Helsinki is where NSAG can test the central governance hypothesis — that conscious, multi-dimensional governance produces measurable nervous system improvement — in the most controlled fashion context available.

Seoul Fashion Week

Dongdaemun Design Plaza · Seoul, South Korea

Seoul is the most explicit case study for attention governance in fashion. K-pop’s attention machine — parasocial celebrity relationships, fan culture designed for maximum engagement, idol-brand-fan governance triangles — runs directly through fashion week. BLACKPINK and BTS members attending as brand ambassadors are the attention governance mechanism itself. Every show at Seoul Fashion Week is simultaneously a fashion event and a fandom activation. The governance of parasocial attention, fan belonging, and the cognitive load of K-fashion’s hyper-accelerated trend cycles is NSAG’s primary research question at Seoul. Hosted at Zaha Hadid’s Dongdaemun Design Plaza — a building that is itself a nervous-system-aware spatial governance argument.

Mar & Oct · Biannual · Tokyo

Rakuten Fashion Week Tokyo

Multiple Venues · Tokyo, Japan · JFWO

Tokyo is the most philosophically coherent fashion governance week. Comme des Garçons (Rei Kawakubo), Yohji Yamamoto, and the Issey Miyake legacy brands are more than collections; they are systematic arguments about how clothing should govern the body’s relationship to space, movement, and identity. Kawakubo’s work in particular has always been a governance argument: fashion as institutional resistance to conventional body governance. Tokyo is also the most progressive fashion week on gender and disability diversity in casting — its governance of who appears on a runway, and in what capacity, is more advanced than any of the Big Four. NSAG’s research here asks: when designers explicitly govern the body’s relationship to space (rather than just dressing it), what measurable changes occur in sensory coherence and agency?

Active: Agency & Consent · Sensory Coherence · Meaning & Purpose · Belonging · Identity

Apr & Oct · Biannual · Shanghai

Shanghai Fashion Week

Multiple Venues · Shanghai, China

The world’s largest fashion week by attendance — 30,000+ buyers and media per season. Shanghai is the most advanced AI-governance case study in fashion: digital twin runways, AI-assisted collection design, and AI-powered buyer matching are being implemented at a scale and speed no other fashion week has approached. The governance question NSAG asks here — who governs the AI that governs the show? — is most urgent at Shanghai. Additionally, Shanghai is the world’s largest luxury counterfeiting market, making trust governance — the authentication of design, brand, and cultural identity — a specific and pressing research dimension. Chinese designers are emerging alongside Western luxury houses, raising economic justice questions about whose aesthetic governance the global fashion market acknowledges.

Active: Attention · Trust · Cognitive Load · Economic Justice · Cultural Safety

May · Annual · Sydney

Australian Fashion Week

Museum of Contemporary Art · Sydney Harbour, Australia

AFW is the most culturally urgent governance conversation in fashion outside West Africa. Now held at the Museum of Contemporary Art against the backdrop of the Sydney Opera House and Harbour Bridge — the most visually spectacular fashion week venue in the world — AFW’s cultural safety governance is at a turning point. First Nations (Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander) designers are claiming governance of their own cultural IP in fashion at a moment when the Australian fashion industry broadly has a long history of appropriating indigenous visual languages without attribution or benefit. MAARA Collective, Ngali, and other First Nations labels are rewriting who has the right to govern the representation of Australian culture in fashion. The AFC’s Global Gateways programme is beginning to formalise this governance internationally.

Lagos Fashion Week

Lagos, Nigeria · Founded by Omoyemi Akerele, 2011

Lagos Fashion Week is simultaneously a commercial event and a governance claim — West African designers asserting ownership of African aesthetic governance at a moment when Western luxury houses (Dior, Louis Vuitton, Valentino) are actively drawing from African visual traditions without attribution, benefit, or accountability. Maki Oh, Lanre Da Silva Ajayi, Fruché, and Orange Culture are building more than brands: they are building a governance infrastructure for who has the right to define, profit from, and develop African fashion. NSAG’s cultural safety and economic justice dimensions are most active here. Lagos is where the governance of cultural IP in fashion — who profits from African aesthetics, and under what terms — is being litigated in practice, on the ground.

São Paulo Fashion Week

São Paulo, Brazil · SPFW · Latin America’s Largest

SPFW is undergoing a governance transition: from a purely commercial fashion week to one with explicit sustainability guidelines, indigenous representation requirements, and Afro-Brazilian visibility mandates. This mirrors the governance question AFW is navigating — when a fashion system that has historically excluded or extracted from specific communities begins formally governing inclusion, does the governance produce genuine belonging or performative compliance? NSAG’s repair and accountability dimension is the research question at SPFW. The ABVTEX sustainability certification framework gives NSAG an existing governance infrastructure to complement with nervous system measurement. Ronaldo Fraga’s work on indigenous and Afro-Brazilian cultural integration is the most NSAG-aligned editorial presence on the SPFW schedule.

Feb · Annual · Dubai

Dubai Fashion Week

Dubai Design District (d3) · UAE · DDFC

Dubai Fashion Week is the most politically complex governance experiment in fashion. It operates simultaneously at: state governance level (UAE’s deliberate fashion investment as economic diversification — Dubai Design District d3 is a government-built infrastructure); cultural governance level (Islamic modest fashion as a formal design category with its own governance traditions — one that has existed and governed itself for centuries without Western fashion week validation); and labour governance level (the Gulf luxury economy runs on South and Southeast Asian migrant labour whose governance conditions are among the most opaque of any fashion supply chain). NSAG’s agency and consent, cultural safety, and economic justice dimensions are all simultaneously active in a context where each has a different sovereign power relationship than in Western fashion markets.

Every professional sports franchise governs the attention, belonging, emotional regulation, and trust of millions of fans — in arenas, on broadcast, through social and apps. The question is whether you govern them intentionally.

Las Vegas Raiders

Allegiant Stadium · Las Vegas Strip Adjacent · $1.9B

The Raiders relocated to Las Vegas to inhabit the world’s most explicitly attention-engineered city — a deliberate governance decision about which environment the franchise would govern from. NSAG’s research question at the Raiders is unique: when a franchise deliberately relocates to a city whose entire governance model is nervous system extraction, and when its fan community already self-governs belonging more explicitly than any other in the league, what does a formal NSAG audit reveal about what’s working and what’s missing?

NHL · Pacific Division · Seattle · Founded 2021

Seattle Kraken

Climate Pledge Arena · World’s First Zero-Carbon Sports Venue

The Seattle Kraken is the most intentionally governance-designed franchise in North American professional sport. Founded in 2021, the Kraken built explicit governance frameworks into their founding documents: indigenous land acknowledgment and partnership with the Duwamish Tribe and other local nations, Climate Pledge Arena as the world’s first certified zero-carbon sports venue (rebuilt from the ground up inside the historic Key Arena shell), and diversity and belonging commitments that exceed any existing NHL mandate. The Kraken built governance in from the very founding, as an original intention. NSAG’s advisory audit of the Kraken answers the rarest question in professional sport: when a franchise builds governance-first from founding, what dimensions did they govern well, what did good intentions miss, and what has three years of operation revealed?

Advisory Target
Rugby Union · Test Rugby · New Zealand

New Zealand All Blacks

Multiple Venues · New Zealand · 77%+ All-Time Win Rate

The All Blacks are NSAG’s most important cultural safety case study in professional sport. The haka — performed before every test match since 1905 — is a pre-game governance ceremony that simultaneously governs: Māori cultural authority (cultural safety), collective team belonging and emotional regulation, and the psychophysiological state of both teams. It is also the most globally recognised cultural governance act in professional sport. NSAG’s governance question is precise: the haka belongs to Māori people. The All Blacks include players who are not Māori. The franchise has built its entire global brand identity on a cultural tradition it does not wholly own. NSAG’s cultural safety dimension asks: what governance framework ensures the haka is performed with appropriate accountability to its source — and what does that accountability structure look like in practice across 120 years of international rugby?
