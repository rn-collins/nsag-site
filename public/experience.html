Part I

The Moment of Recognition

The assessment is the vehicle for a specific experience: the moment when an institutional decision-maker realizes they have been producing a physiological effect on the people in their institution without knowing it.

"I didn't know I was doing that to people."

That moment is the entire product. Everything downstream — the advisory engagement, the CE, the Brief subscription, the follow-up — is support for someone who has already had that realization and wants to act on it. If the assessment creates that moment, NSAG has done its core work.

What creates the moment

What a scoring tool asks

"Does your institution have a standardized cannabis screening instrument in place?"

You think about the institution. You answer yes or no. You receive a score. The score is abstract. You are not moved.

What NSAG asks

"Think of a patient in your care right now who uses cannabis daily. Do you know about it? Almost certainly not. Here is what that costs their safety — and yours."

You think about a specific person. You answer a short set of questions. The score lands as confirmation of something you already recognized. You are moved.

Three things that produce the recognition

Before the questions
The implication is named first
The governance implication frames the assessment. A person who reads what is happening to people in their institution because of a governance gap — before answering any questions — experiences the score as evidence of something they already understood. That is the recognition sequence. Score-then-implication produces a verdict. Implication-then-score produces a recognition.
In the questions
Each question teaches
Every question carries implicit information. Answering "no" to "Does your institution have a proactive language identification protocol in place before client self-identification is required?" tells you something you did not know about what your intake design is asking of your clients. The act of answering should feel like learning.
In the results
The tier report names the people affected
The tier report opens with the specific people affected at this tier level. "Your institution is EARLY STAGE in Disclosure Safety Infrastructure" is a verdict. "In your institution today, the design of your intake process is telling patients that it is not safe to disclose cannabis use. An unknown number of them are not telling you. You are managing their pharmacology without their full history." — that is a recognition.

What the immediate tier email looks like

From: NSAG · Neurocognitive Systems Advisory Group
Your M9 score — and what it means for one patient in your care today
Right now in your institution

In your institution today, the design of your intake process is telling patients that it is not safe to disclose cannabis use. Lapham et al. (2022) found that 35.1% of primary care patients reported medical cannabis use on a confidential survey while only 4.8% had it documented in the EHR — from the same patient population, measured simultaneously. Your score puts you in that gap. An unknown number of your patients are managing their pharmacology without their provider having the full picture.

One action for this week

Add one question to your intake form today: "Do you currently use cannabis, CBD, or hemp products?" You do not need EHR integration, a committee, or a policy change to do this. You need a form edit and 48 hours. That one question is the beginning of closing the gap.

What PIONEERING looks like in M9

A PIONEERING institution has a validated cannabis screening instrument at every patient contact with ≥85% completion, all clinical providers trained in CYP450 interaction protocols, standardized EHR cannabis fields with ≥85% completion, a written disclosure safety policy communicated at every intake, documented legal guidance updated within 3 years, and cannabis documentation rate tracked as a quarterly QI metric.

Part III

One-Action Library

For every module at every tier: one specific, zero-budget, no-committee-required action that one person can take this week and see a visible result within 7 days.

These are starting actions. They are designed to create the first observable change — the moment when abstract governance language becomes something a specific person experienced differently.

M9 Early Stage
Add one cannabis screening question to your intake form today. 'Do you currently use cannabis, CBD, or hemp products?' No EHR integration, no committee, no policy change required. Form edit + 48 hours.
M9 Developing
Email your EHR administrator today: what would it take to add a cannabis-specific structured field to the intake workflow? Get a timeline in writing.
M9 Emerging
Set a 30-day cannabis documentation rate goal. Pull your current documentation rate from your EHR. Write the number down. That is your baseline. Send it to one clinical leader.
M8 Early Stage
Write down the answer to: how many patient contacts does each clinical staff member have per shift? If you don't know, that is the governance gap. The first action is learning the number.
M8 Developing
Schedule a 30-minute staff meeting in the next 2 weeks. Agenda: 'What is the one thing about your workload that leadership cannot currently see?' Listen. Do not problem-solve in the meeting. Document what you hear.
M1 Early Stage
Today, read one document in your client intake package at the reading level of your most vulnerable client. Count the syllables in the words. That is where you are in plain language governance.
M1 Developing
Identify the one client-facing document with the highest reading level. Send it to your communications team or a plain language reviewer today with a request for a revised version within 30 days.
M2 Early Stage
Today, ask: has any AI tool influencing decisions at this institution been independently assessed for demographic disparities in outcomes? If the answer is 'I don't know,' that is the answer. The first action is finding out.
M2 Developing
Request the technical documentation for the primary AI risk assessment tool in use. Specifically request: the validation cohort demographics, the false-positive rate by racial/ethnic group, and any known limitations for your specific population.
M11 Early Stage
Today, name every AI tool that influences a clinical decision at your institution. If you cannot name them all, that is the governance gap. The first action is making the complete list.
M11 Developing
For the primary clinical AI tool in use: pull 3 months of outcome data and ask your data team to stratify it by race and ethnicity. If they cannot do this today, that is also a governance finding.
M12 Early Stage
Walk through one learning space this week. Measure ambient noise at three points using a free smartphone app. Note access to natural light. Write down what you find. That is your first built environment assessment.
M12 Developing
Identify the one classroom or learning space in your institution most likely to activate threat responses in students who have experienced adverse childhood events. Name specifically what feature would activate them. That naming is the beginning of governance.
M13 Early Stage
If your institution offers any traditional or complementary medicine practice: does a written agreement exist with the community or practitioner specifying attribution, compensation, and scope of use? If not, identify who needs to be in the room to create one.
M5 Early Stage
Today, walk through your institution and note: (1) visible natural light sources, (2) visible plants or natural materials, (3) views of outdoor natural environments. Score each on a 1–5 scale. Send the three scores to one colleague. That is your biophilic audit.
M6 Early Stage
Today, answer: does your institution have a written policy specifying what corporate sponsorship it will not accept? If not, that is the governance gap. Name three categories that should be in an exclusion list and send them to your legal or governance team.
M10 Early Stage
Use a free smartphone decibel meter app to measure noise in your busiest patient care area at the busiest time of day. Compare to the hospital noise guideline of ≤35 dBA (WHO daytime ward guideline). Write down the gap. That is your acoustic governance baseline.
M3 Early Stage
If your institution offers any psychedelic-assisted services: can you name, right now, what your adverse event response protocol is for a psychological crisis — a psychological crisis, specifically? If not, that protocol does not exist. The first action is writing it.
M7 Early Stage
Identify one built environment decision made in the past 12 months by your planning department or governing body. Ask: was a mental health impact assessment conducted? If not, draft a one-paragraph proposal for what that assessment would have asked.
M4 Early Stage
Today: how many of your clinical providers have received training on cannabis-opioid interactions in the past 2 years? If you don't know, that is the answer. Email your medical education team today asking for the number.
The Contract

What you go in knowing. What you leave with.

Going in, you will know exactly what you will get.

  • A score across six governance dimensions — specific and concrete
  • A recognition of what that score means for a specific person in your institution right now
  • One action you can take this week — zero budget, no committee required
  • A concrete picture of what PIONEERING looks like in your module
  • A reason to come back in 30 days and measure what changed

Leaving, you will feel one thing you cannot unfeel.

The governance framework is the explanation for why the problem exists. The tier report is the evidence of where your institution is. But what moves people — what compels them to forward the report to a colleague, to call the discovery line, to bring NSAG to a board meeting — is something more specific.

It is the image of one person: a patient whose warfarin dose is subtly wrong because their cannabis use is undocumented. A defendant whose bail was set by an algorithm that has never been assessed for disparate impact in this jurisdiction. A student whose classroom noise level suppresses the prefrontal access required for the test they are about to take.

That image is specific enough to share. The story of what changes when the institution governs that effect — that is what compels.
