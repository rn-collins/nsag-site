NSAG / Field Research
Primary Research · 2027–2029

NSAG in the World.

NSAG's framework does not live only in research papers. It is tested in the rooms where institutional decisions are actually made — at conferences, design weeks, clinical symposia, and the field sites where governance meets lived experience. This is the program of field work underpinning the dataset NSAG is building toward 1,000 completed Governance Challenges by 2030.

What Field Research Means Here

The Governance Challenge in context.

Every field event on this calendar is an opportunity to deploy NSAG's Governance Challenge — the diagnostic instrument that produces a scored, documented picture of how an institution's physical, procedural, and digital environment is affecting the nervous systems of people inside it.

Field research is not attendance at conferences. It is administering governance assessments at institutional sites, conducting primary research interviews with the individuals building the organizations in each of NSAG's 15 module domains, and collecting the dataset rows that make peer-reviewed publication possible.

The target: 1,000 completed Governance Challenges across all 15 modules by December 2030. The data collected in field supports every other NSAG product — the advisories, the continuing education programs, and the published research.

How a field event works
01
Pre-event institutional outreach — identify 3–5 organizations attending that match NSAG module domains.
02
Administer Governance Challenge at the institutional site or via structured field interview during the event window.
03
Deliver Governance Brief (8–12 pages) + Dimension Memo to the participating institution within 30 days.
04
Dataset row added. Institution receives analysis. NSAG advances toward 1,000 completed Challenges.
The data exchange

In 2026–2027, NSAG field partnerships are zero-cost to institutions. Organizations receive governance analysis and scored feedback. NSAG receives a completed Governance Challenge dataset row. No retainer, no contract — a research partnership.
