M7 Civic & Built Environment

Conscious Cities

Urban planning is health governance. Every zoning decision, development approval, and infrastructure investment shapes the mental health of the people living in that environment. Most planning processes have no mechanism for governing those effects.

Take the Free Assessment → See the Evidence
The Governance Problem

Why This Module Exists

Evans (2003) reviewed the research literature linking built environment to mental health outcomes and established that neighborhood disorder, housing quality, noise, and green space are independent mental health determinants: environmental conditions that produce mental health outcomes rather than simply correlating with them. Sarkar et al. (2018) confirmed the equity dimension in 94,879 UK Biobank participants: the protective effect of residential greenness is strongest in lower-income populations. Münzel et al. (2014) documented the physiological mechanism for noise: cortisol elevation, sleep architecture fragmentation, and cardiovascular stress pathway activation. The populations most harmed by poor built environment governance are those least able to compensate through private means.

Six Governance Dimensions

What the Assessment Measures

Each readiness assessment scores your institution across these six governance dimensions on a five-point scale, producing a tier report (PIONEERING, EMERGING, DEVELOPING, or EARLY STAGE).

PIONEERING Governance

What the Highest Tier Looks Like

PIONEERING — Fully Implemented Governance

Mental health impact assessment required for all built environment decisions above defined threshold; documented minimum green space access standard with compliance tracking; noise monitoring with governance response protocol; housing quality standards with enforcement partnership; formal community participation mechanism with composition specified; equity impact assessment required for all major decisions.

The full tier operationalization — PIONEERING through EARLY STAGE, with specific observable criteria for all six dimensions — is documented in the Framework Foundation.

And anyone who lives in the city being planned. Every zoning, noise, and green-space decision lands on a nervous system, and lands hardest on those least able to move away.

Space Law Application

Applied to Space and Extreme Environments

M7 (Conscious Cities) is the module whose space law application is the most structurally direct. Evans (2003) established that neighborhood disorder, housing quality, noise, and green space are independent mental health determinants that act as causes of mental health outcomes rather than mere correlates. In a spacecraft, every one of those variables is constructed. The ‘city’ is entirely designed. Every acoustic environment, every light source, every social space is a governance decision. Remove the option to leave, remove Earth from the frame, and Conscious Cities governance becomes Conscious Spacecraft governance — with the same evidence base and higher consequences.
